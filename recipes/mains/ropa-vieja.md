# Ropa Vieja (Cuban Shredded Beef)

Small-batch version, braised in the oven.

## Ingredients

- 1 piece of beef (small batch)
- Adobo seasoning and black pepper
- Avocado oil
- 1 can tomato sauce
- 1–2 boxes beef bone broth (start with 1½)
- 3–4 big spoonfuls of minced garlic (jar in the fridge door)
- 3–4 scoops of cumin with the ½ tsp measuring spoon (1½–2 tsp total)
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 beef bouillon cube (package in the drawer)
- 1 whole onion, roughly chopped
- Peppers and more onions, added later

## Equipment

- Black Le Creuset pot with lid (used for searing and braising)

## Steps

1. **Prep:** Pat the meat dry on both sides with paper towels.
2. **Season:** A good sprinkle of adobo and black pepper on both sides. Go light on the adobo, since the bouillon and other ingredients add salt.
3. **Sear:** In the black Le Creuset pot, brown both sides in avocado oil with the burner on 4.5. Everything else goes into this same pot.
4. **Build the braise:** Add the tomato sauce, then enough beef bone broth to cover the meat. Less broth makes it more tomato-forward. Add the garlic, cumin, bay leaf, bouillon cube, and chopped onion. Don't add more adobo yet.
5. **First bake:** Lid on, oven at 300°F for around 3 hours.
6. **Check:** Stir and see if the meat is starting to break apart. If it's cooking too fast, drop the heat to around 280–285°F. Taste and adjust the seasoning; it usually needs more cumin and garlic.
7. **Second bake:** Once the meat pulls apart, add the peppers and onions and bake for about 2 more hours.
8. **Finish:** Adjust the seasoning again, adding salt or adobo last. If it's too thick, add more broth.

## Notes

- **Fond:** Searing leaves brown, crusty bits stuck to the bottom of the pot. These are called *fond* (French, pronounced roughly "fohn"). When you pour in the tomato sauce and broth, the fond loosens and dissolves into the liquid, adding a lot of flavor. That's a big reason to sear and braise in the same pot. Scraping the bottom with a wooden spoon as you add the liquid helps release it.
- **Salt:** You can always add salt but can't take it out, so hold off on extra adobo until the end.
- **Temperature:** 300°F is confirmed. Lower it if the meat cooks too quickly.
